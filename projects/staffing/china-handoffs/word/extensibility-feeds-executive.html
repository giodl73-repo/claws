Extensibility and Feeds

Collaboration and ownership opportunities for Scout, MOS3, ClawHub, and OpenClaw

Omar's vision: Build the platform for plugins, skills, UX extensions, governance, ratings, versioning, tenant controls, and first-party/community contribution.

Purpose

This brief is a starting point for discussing where the Extensibility team may want to contribute or assume ownership. It is not a fixed assignment. The portfolio can be divided among Scout rollout, MOS3 platform contracts, publisher feeds, extension governance, and reliability according to team charter and capacity.

Product vision

Scout should provide one coherent extension lifecycle across discovery, publishing, following, installation, updates, trust, and tenant governance for plugins, skills, MCP servers, connectors, and future extension types.

  • OpenClaw owns generic, community-usable runtime and client primitives.
  • ClawHub owns community catalog and publisher-feed services.
  • MOS3/MAC remain authoritative for Microsoft's registry, acquisitions, package metadata, package defense, and effective feed composition.
  • Lobster/Scout integrates these systems into the Microsoft-hosted product, UX, E2E, rollout, and support experience.
Guardrail: Do not create a parallel Lobster catalog, equate following with trust or installation, or make community OpenClaw and ClawHub depend on Microsoft services.

Suggested first conversation

  1. Choose one initial seam: Scout rollout, MOS3 contracts, publisher feeds, governance, or reliability.
  2. Confirm a coordinating DRI and partner contacts without assuming one team implements the whole portfolio.
  3. Recheck the live PRs and select a bounded first deliverable.
  4. Agree on the E2E and operational proof required before broader enablement.

Success measures

  • Scout feeds are safely enabled with real FirstRelease proof.
  • MOS3 owns explicit feed and RBAC contracts and remains the production authority.
  • Publisher feeds have a current producer and useful OpenClaw consumer.
  • Users and admins can explain source, revision, approval, trust, package defense, freshness, drift, and blocked reasons.
  • Community OpenClaw/ClawHub remain independently useful.
