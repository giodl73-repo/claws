Policy and Compliance

Managed OpenClaw governance collaboration opportunities

Omar's Admin & Governance vision: Own tenant/global settings, policy, permissions, approvals, audit/override state, Partner Center, marketplace governance, and M365 auth decisions.

Purpose

This brief focuses the policy portion of the Admin & Governance vision. It is not a fixed assignment. Admin & Governance, Security, Infrastructure, Data, and Frontend may lead or co-own different seams: policy composition, evidence ingestion, central compliance, enforcement, UX, and fleet operations.

Product vision

Every managed OpenClaw instance should have a centrally visible, attributable, and actionable compliance state. Administrators should know which policy is effective, whether the instance evaluated that exact policy, what warnings or violations exist, what is enforced, and how to remediate or grant a bounded exception.

  • OpenClaw owns generic policy evaluation, evidence, findings, doctor behavior, and repair classifications.
  • Lobster/Microsoft control plane selects and materializes enterprise policy, ingests verified evidence, enforces hosted outcomes, and provides fleet/tenant experiences.
  • MOS3/MAC contribute authoritative feed, package-defense, tenant, and availability decisions.
Guardrail: Missing identity, policy, evidence, or a mandatory enforcement dependency must never appear compliant or silently permit a protected high-risk action. Lower-risk reporting failures should degrade visibly without crashing the OpenClaw runtime.

Work still to be done

  • Define the effective-policy and evidence contract across deployment/global, tenant, selected-feed, and personal baselines.
  • Enforce package-defense/quarantine outcomes consistently while preserving distinct warning, scanning, trust, vulnerability, revocation, and exception reasons.
  • Ingest OpenClaw conformance evidence for the exact materialized policy/config revision and reject missing, invalid, mismatched, stale, or failed evidence.
  • Implement durable states: compliant, compliantWithWarnings, noncompliant, stale, unknown, and excepted.
  • Decide which findings block startup, activation, discovery, installation, or a specific operation and which only warn.
  • Provide fleet/tenant UX for findings, source, reason, freshness, repair class, acknowledgement, exception, expiry, owner, reversal, and history.
  • Add measured runtime budgets, cancellation, failure isolation, fleet/ring rollout, partial-failure recovery, E2E, telemetry, dashboards, SIEM, incident response, and privacy/retention.
  • Produce timestamped, attributable, tamper-evident evidence exports usable for audit and investigation.

Suggested first conversation

  1. Select one seam and a coordinating DRI; implementation can remain distributed.
  2. Decide which system owns the durable compliance record and evidence export.
  3. Define the first lifecycle enforcement point and its severity/failure posture.
  4. Choose a bounded E2E: compliant, warning, violation, stale evidence, exception, repair, rollback, and recovery.

Success measures

  • Every managed instance reports verified evidence for its exact effective policy/config revision.
  • Missing, stale, invalid, or failed evidence never appears green.
  • Package-defense and policy outcomes have consistent enforcement, explicit exceptions, and actionable reasons.
  • Administrators can explain compliance, warnings, violations, drift, freshness, exceptions, and remediation.
  • Evidence is tamper-evident and exportable; runtime behavior remains stable and bounded.
