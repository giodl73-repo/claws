LobsterClaw is moving toward a qualified 6.33 cutover for Lobster this week

WHY THIS PROJECT EXISTS
Lobster needs Microsoft-required OpenClaw capabilities before every change is available upstream, but a large patch queue is hard to reproduce, govern, test, and retire safely. LobsterClaw turns that queue into an immutable private distribution: one signed OpenClaw base, byte-identical patches with provenance and exit conditions, and one generated source tree that can be rebuilt exactly for every stable release.

THE OPERATING MODEL
RFCs and upstream PRs enter a separate candidate ledger first. We decide whether to watch, evaluate, carry, or supersede each item; only explicitly admitted patches enter a release generation. As OpenClaw adopts the behavior, we remove the downstream carry instead of becoming a permanent fork.

WHAT LANDED TODAY

WHY THE TESTS ARE TAKING REAL WORK
We are not hiding incompatibilities or weakening gates. The complete patch composition exposed stale TypeScript fixtures, generated-release drift, strict lint debt, and mirror-sensitive shrinkwrap behavior that individual patches did not reveal alone. Each deterministic failure becomes a provenance-bound repair patch, then the entire immutable tree is replayed and tested again.
