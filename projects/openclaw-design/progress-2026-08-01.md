## Practical Read

### Design notebook

Roughly four fifths through the current notebook-design program:

- inventory, owner audits, enterprise grading, and relationship modeling are
  done;
- all six ranked evidence contracts are framed; and
- the remaining work is narrower and proof-oriented.

This estimate describes notebook readiness, not upstream product completion.

### Evidence program

All framed, none proved:

- `6/6` ranked slices have source-backed designs;
- `0/6` have executable conformance fixtures;
- partial and unknown outcomes still need proof; and
- the proposed common evidence header remains experimental.

### Upstream readiness

Early:

- the material is suitable for selecting focused RFC candidates;
- it is not yet suitable for filing one umbrella architecture RFC; and
- at least two unlike executable slices should validate the evidence envelope
  before proposing a shared contract.

## What Is Strongest

- Owner boundaries and rejected universal abstractions.
- Current-source coverage of the major runtime owner domains.
- Identity/lifetime and cross-facility fencing vocabulary.
- Enterprise gap grading separated from runtime maturity.
- Release/update compatibility and node/device trust modeling.
- Five concrete evidence slices with explicit deletion targets and disproof
  conditions.

## What Is Still Weak

- No running conformance fixture proves the proposed evidence shapes.
- Session export/deletion remains copy-inventory-heavy even after separating
  export creation from deletion propagation.
- Several mapped support areas remain project references rather than
  source-audited facility specs:
  catalog, extensions/composition, scheduling, state continuity, hosting,
  policy, diagnostics, signals/audit, localization, and hosting integration.
- The evidence envelope has not been tested through partial settlement,
  restart, retry, or schema evolution.
