A closer look at OpenClaw dependency management
Jeff Teper mentioned dependency management yesterday, so we took a closer look at OpenClaw. Quick question: how many dependencies do you think it has? Turns out: 2,624.

What we did and learned
We built a reproducible inventory, traced direct and transitive use, separated runtime from build/test exposure, and reviewed vulnerability, maintenance, license, provenance, feed admission, and governance evidence. The result: 33 High, 1,707 Moderate, 878 Low, six Unknown, and no Critical findings. Roughly 98.5% are Low or Moderate, so this is a tractable remediation program—not an emergency dependency crisis.

The foundation is good
OpenClaw already has security-owner approval for dependency changes, Dependabot, release-age protection, lock checks, production vulnerability blocking, dependency reports, npm release evidence, and signed container SBOM/provenance. We should strengthen this system rather than add Microsoft-specific process upstream.

What comes next
We will split the highest-confidence package, WebRTC, and image fixes; add focused hardening for ecosystem update coverage, dependency ownership/pins, and governed release-age exclusions; and complete Lobster’s product-exact Component Governance attestation.
