A proposal for the team

A managed bridge from our OpenClaw release to upstream.

Here is a practical solution to a challenge we are facing: we need fixes and new capabilities before our next OpenClaw upgrade, but every one-off downstream patch increases drift and makes the next rebase harder to understand.

The proposal: use the controlled fork as our transition surface.

Every item we carry gets an identity, owner, status, dependency, proof, rollback path, upstream replacement, and deletion condition. Nothing enters as an anonymous patch that must live forever.

1. Contribute once.
A team member opens a fork PR containing the change, ledger entry, and focused proof.

2. Promote the exact result.
A linked Lobster PR consumes that exact fork SHA or artifact and validates the same generation in the image.

3. Use upstream fixes early.
If OpenClaw fixes something after our current version, we carry the exact upstream PR now and record the first release that contains it.

4. Rebase and remove the carry.
When we reach that OpenClaw release, the retained regression proves the upstream fix and the temporary carry is deleted.

5. Repeat from any pinned baseline.
The fork is reconstructable and rebaseable. We recently exercised this process against OpenClaw 6.33; once the team is there, it becomes the managed path for future patches and RFC implementations.
