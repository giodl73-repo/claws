# Module 09 Private Source Audit

This is private operating context. Do not copy private provenance, generated
statistics, employer terminology, level codes, named people, protected data, or
identifying examples into LIFT.

## Source concepts reviewed

- unequal access to manager, next-level, and senior advocacy;
- remote and proximity effects;
- nonlinear careers and leave;
- gender, race, discipline, and organizational-network hypotheses;
- candidate and employee scoring;
- bias corrections and subgroup calibration;
- opportunity distribution;
- privacy, surveillance, and protected information;
- anti-retaliation and safe voice;
- automated or AI-supported inference.

## Retain through rewrite

- advocacy opportunities and informal networks are not equally distributed;
- identical work can receive different visibility, attribution, and support;
- proximity, schedule, role, and network structure can affect evidence
  opportunity without changing contribution quality;
- ambiguous criteria and unstructured judgment can reproduce bias;
- aggregate distribution review can reveal pathway concentration;
- people need safe correction and protected concern routes;
- privacy and power affect whether evidence can be gathered responsibly;
- a framework should specify prohibited uses, not only intended uses.

## Remove

- hiring or promotion classifiers based on inferred M/S/E;
- remote, gender, race, discipline, career-gap, or nonlinear-path score
  corrections;
- generated subgroup accuracy, penalty, correlation, and success estimates;
- claims that protected identity determines an individual's true score;
- race-by-role, gender-by-discipline, or demographic trajectory formulas;
- protected or private characteristics in an individual LIFT record;
- scraping communication, calendars, social networks, or profiles to infer
  advocacy;
- using group disparities to alter an individual's score arithmetically;
- using silence, refusal, or limited visibility as evidence of low commitment;
- treating manager consent requests as freely voluntary under power;
- score dashboards for hiring, pay, promotion, performance, succession,
  termination, layoff, retention risk, or workforce ranking;
- AI-generated personnel judgments from digital exhaust.

## Public rewrite thesis

A LIFT score may still describe an absent advocacy path under unequal
opportunity. It is unusable for comparing people, judging capability, or making
employment decisions. Evidence collected unsafely or used for an unauthorized
purpose must be restricted, discarded, or routed.

The public module should:

- put safety and purpose before scoring;
- define allowed, restricted, and prohibited uses;
- distinguish an advocacy observation from a judgment of capability;
- inspect opportunity distribution without applying demographic score
  corrections;
- keep individual development and aggregate equity audit in separate lanes;
- prohibit protected-characteristic inference;
- define purpose limitation, data minimization, access, correction, retention,
  and deletion;
- recognize that consent alone is insufficient under workplace power;
- provide anti-retaliation and formal-routing conditions;
- prohibit automated employment decisions and hidden scoring;
- govern AI and tool use;
- make misuse response explicit.

## External comparison

Public research and authoritative guidance support adjacent claims:

- formal merit language does not eliminate biased reward allocation;
- equal performance ratings can produce unequal career rewards;
- network structures and returns can differ by gender;
- psychological safety and fear shape employee voice and silence;
- workplace monitoring creates privacy and power concerns;
- workplace data processing requires purpose, proportionality, access,
  correction, security, retention, and accountability;
- algorithmic hiring and management claims often exceed their evidence and can
  reproduce organizational bias.

No public source reviewed validates LIFT as a hiring, promotion, pay,
performance, succession, termination, retention-risk, or workforce-selection
instrument.
