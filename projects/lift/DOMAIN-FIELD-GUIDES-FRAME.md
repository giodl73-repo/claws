# LIFT Domain Field Guides Frame

## Frame

### Thesis

```text
working LIFT modules and companion practices
+ missing translation for different authority and evidence systems
-> portable field guides without domain-specific scoring
```

The affected readers work outside the implicit artifact-rich, salaried,
office-management setting. Their evidence may be schedule-bound, protected,
distributed across organizations, constrained by professional duties, or
carried through informal authority.

The shared capability is a domain-guide contract that translates:

- ordinary work evidence;
- actual M/S/E authority;
- common pathway distortion;
- one feasible bounded action;
- records that must not be reused;
- qualified processes that remain authoritative.

Modules 00-10 retain all LIFT semantics, scoring, evidence, and ethical
authority.

### V1 boundaries

- seven compact work-setting guides;
- no domain score, correction factor, benchmark, or profile type;
- no legal, regulatory, professional, clinical, or employment interpretation;
- no claim of domain effectiveness;
- no new templates beyond one optional shared lens;
- no assumption that an industry name determines its operating model.

### Simplification target

Avoid Module 11, repeated domain caveats in every module, and separate versions
of LIFT. Retire or split any guide that does not change a concrete evidence,
authority, safety, or action decision.

### Disproof

The design should be revised if domain practitioners find that a guide:

- misidentifies real authority;
- requires inaccessible or protected evidence;
- adds more documentation than it removes;
- offers no feasible action for the reader's schedule or leverage;
- interferes with qualified reporting or professional duties;
- merely repeats generic LIFT language.

## Audit

Existing LIFT already:

- preserves `unknown` and prohibits score arithmetic;
- accounts for shift, field, remote, contract, and unequal-access conditions;
- prohibits digital-exhaust inference and protected-data reuse;
- separates qualified investigations and legal or employment processes;
- provides reader guides, stories, and cross-cutting practices as non-module
  layers.

The missing surface was a consistent translation of those rules into
recognizable work settings.

## Compare

### Internal analogues

| Analogue | Disposition | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| reader guides | reuse | situation routing without changing module authority |
| cross-cutting practices | reuse | compose existing rules around recurring problems |
| stories and metaphors | adapt | consistent card structure and explicit stop conditions |
| new core modules | avoid | domain differences do not create new advocacy dimensions |
| domain-specific scoring | avoid | would introduce unsupported comparability and correction |

### External boundary comparators

Official OSHA, HHS, Department of Education, OPM, Department of Labor, IRS, and
eCFR materials were used only to confirm that safety, privacy, records,
classification, and governance systems retain authority. They do not validate
LIFT or provide universal legal rules.

## Evaluate

The 17-role review closed major risks involving:

- domain stereotypes;
- layer overlap and double counting;
- protected service and customer records;
- activity metrics as proxy scores;
- US examples as universal law;
- documentation and guide proliferation.

One minor defer remains: care, education, and public service may need separate
guides if practitioners demonstrate materially different LIFT actions.

The seven-scenario synthetic test added three cross-domain corrections:

- evidence requests need time, authorized devices or channels, language, and
  accessibility support;
- professional and public duties do not wait for a favorable M/S/E profile;
- overlapping authority must not be presented as independent pathways.

No scenario required another domain guide or a split of the combined
care/education/public-service guide.

## Slice

The public slice is:

- one shared guide contract;
- seven compact field guides spanning different evidence and authority
  conditions;
- one synthetic example and one stop boundary per guide;
- one source-boundary page;
- one synthetic role-review record.

The proof gate is independent use by practitioners in the named settings.
