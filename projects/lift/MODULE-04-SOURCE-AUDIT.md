# Module 04 Private Source Audit

This is private operating context. Do not copy private provenance, generated
statistics, employer terminology, level codes, or identifying examples into
LIFT.

## Source concepts reviewed

- the transition from individual contribution to people management;
- changes in the evidence used to understand manager effectiveness;
- reliance on familiar technical or specialist work;
- psychological safety, clarity, development, credit, and autonomy;
- team experience as evidence about management;
- the first months of a manager transition;
- management as an identity transition.

## Retain through rewrite

- prior individual excellence does not automatically constitute evidence of
  management effectiveness;
- the primary unit of work shifts toward creating conditions the team can use;
- new managers may retreat into familiar personal execution when management
  work is ambiguous or less immediately rewarding;
- clarity, feedback, development, attribution, and decision authority are
  observable management practices;
- management identity develops through practice, feedback, and social
  interaction rather than title alone;
- organizations and the manager's own manager share responsibility for a
  supported transition;
- team experience and team outcomes matter, but neither should become a
  simplistic score or a total causal judgment.

## Remove

- a fixed M reset at transition;
- universal 30-, 60-, or 90-day score trajectories;
- numerical recovery speeds, behavior weights, correlations, and prediction
  ratios;
- the private "IC Ghost" label and diagnostic threshold;
- fixed percentages of time that define management failure;
- claims that prior individual contribution stops mattering;
- claims that all trust resets to zero;
- promotion gates and level-specific formulas;
- team-size formulas and automatic senior visibility;
- team surveys, attrition, or silence treated as direct proof of manager quality;
- claims that management is the necessary or superior advancement path.

## Public rewrite thesis

The new-manager transition changes the primary evidence of work:

```text
from what I personally produced
to the conditions the team can understand, decide, learn, deliver, and sustain
```

This is a shift in emphasis, not a ban on direct contribution.

The public module should:

- define observable management work;
- help managers recognize the output refuge without diagnosing intent;
- use repeatable transition passes rather than a universal clock;
- establish contracts with the team and the manager's own manager;
- separate inherited conditions from current management acts;
- preserve craft and player-coach realities;
- treat declining or leaving management as a valid decision;
- avoid converting team experience into a hidden promotion score.

## External comparison

Public research supports adjacent claims:

- first-time management involves identity work and iterative learning;
- leadership identity is socially constructed, not created by title alone;
- empowering leadership and delegation can support performance and creativity,
  with contextual limits;
- psychological safety supports learning and speaking up;
- feedback effects vary by design and focus;
- clear goals can improve performance when goals and context are appropriate.

No public source reviewed validates a fixed score reset, transition clock,
behavioral weight, or promotion effect.
