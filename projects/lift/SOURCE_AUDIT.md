# LIFT Private Source Audit

This file is private operating context. Do not copy its provenance, repository
names, paths, or identifying examples into the public LIFT repository.

## Source hierarchy

| Source | Use | Public disposition |
|---|---|---|
| `giodl_microsoft/career`, `gravity/` | Primary conceptual source | Rewrite concepts and observable behaviors |
| `giodl_microsoft/career-gravity` | Earlier research-heavy lineage | Compare history; do not treat generated studies as empirical evidence |
| `giodl_microsoft/merit_plugin` and `career/merit` | Promotion and performance-document machinery | Exclude except for independently generalizable management questions |

The newer combined `career` repository is the working source. The separate
`career-gravity` repository is useful for history, but not a public upstream.

## Preserve through substantial rewrite

- M/S/E as manager, next-level, and executive/senior-sponsor advocacy;
- a 1–10 evidence rubric;
- the weakest dimension as a diagnostic constraint;
- scoring against observable advocacy, not warmth or optimism;
- trajectory, momentum, structural resistance, and changing context;
- the manager transition from personal output to team enablement;
- two-way evidence: leadership support upward and team trust downward;
- managers as amplifiers or bottlenecks for recognition and opportunity;
- credit attribution, named advocacy, growth investment, autonomy, clarity, and
  psychological safety;
- explicit learning and revision after new evidence.

## Rewrite or remove

- employer names, products, organizations, role titles, level codes, internal
  programs, and calibration processes;
- promotion packets, reward guidance, private inputs, cached extracts, and
  generated personal assessments;
- fixed promotion thresholds presented as universal law;
- precise timing, probability, correlation, compensation, and accuracy claims
  derived from simulations or generated vignettes;
- invented journals, simulated expert quotations, and synthetic panels
  presented as external validation;
- claims that advocacy determines outcomes independently of performance,
  opportunity, discrimination, organizational design, or economic conditions;
- advice that encourages favoritism, political manipulation, surveillance, or
  unsupported inference about another person's private relationships.

## Evidence reclassification

| Private claim type | Public treatment |
|---|---|
| Observable management behavior | Retain as practice or diagnostic question |
| Gio's accumulated management lesson | Label `practitioner hypothesis` |
| Generated scenario or simulation | Label `synthetic model` and publish only if it teaches a general mechanism |
| Independently sourced research | Cite the primary or authoritative public source |
| Employer-specific policy or threshold | Remove |
| Exact unsupported statistic | Remove until independently reproduced |

## Initial public thesis

People do not experience management only through their direct relationship.
Their work and growth depend on whether support, context, credit, and advocacy
travel through several organizational layers. LIFT makes those pathways
inspectable through M/S/E without claiming that a score determines a person's
worth or guarantees an outcome.

## First module

Module 00 defines:

- what M, S, and E mean across organizations;
- what a 1–10 score does and does not represent;
- the evidence record behind a score;
- the weakest-link diagnostic;
- limitations, uncertainty, and ethical use.

No original numerical Gravity claim is admitted into Module 00 without
independent public evidence.
