# Module 01 Private Source Audit

This is private operating context. Do not copy private repository names,
employer terminology, generated statistics, or source examples into LIFT.

## Source concepts reviewed

- self-assessment walkthrough for M/S/E;
- fixed numerical "calibration corrections";
- mentor-versus-sponsor distinction;
- the question asking for a recent example of advocacy in one's absence;
- interpretation of vague, specific, defensive, and absent answers;
- proposed repeated assessment cadence.

## Retain through rewrite

- people observe only part of an advocacy relationship;
- warmth, feedback, visibility, mentorship, and sponsorship are different
  evidence classes;
- asking for a specific recent example is more useful than asking for general
  reassurance;
- evidence should be dated, triangulated, and revisable;
- M, S, and E have different observability;
- the strongest useful output may be `unknown`.

## Remove

- exact average over- or under-estimation values;
- automatic downward or upward score adjustments;
- employer levels, calibration meetings, promotion thresholds, and fixed gates;
- claims that the sponsor question has a quantified correction effect;
- inference that evasion proves retention suppression or hidden opposition;
- instructions to escalate solely because a manager answers defensively;
- assumptions that unseen senior advocacy probably exists;
- score sums and deterministic next actions.

## Public rewrite thesis

An M/S/E score is a hypothesis built from incomplete evidence. Honest
assessment improves the evidence record and confidence before it changes the
number.

The sponsor question becomes:

> Can you share a recent example of how you represented my work, growth, or
> scope in a conversation I was not part of?

Its answer supplies one evidence item. It does not reveal private intent,
guarantee advocacy, or prove the absence of support.

## External comparison

Public literature supports adjacent propositions:

- self-assessment can diverge from externally observed evidence;
- feedback seeking is a workplace learning behavior shaped by leadership and
  context;
- sponsorship differs from mentorship through active use of social and
  organizational capital;
- low psychological safety can make direct feedback seeking costly.

None of those sources validates a universal M/S/E correction formula.
