# Module 05 Private Source Audit

This is private operating context. Do not copy private provenance, generated
statistics, employer terminology, level codes, or identifying examples into
LIFT.

## Source concepts reviewed

- manager evaluation from upward and downward perspectives;
- team trust and team-health evidence;
- surveys, attrition, skip-level conversations, and delivery patterns;
- psychological safety, clarity, growth, credit, and autonomy;
- manager reputation compared with team experience;
- manager selection based on former-report outcomes.

## Retain through rewrite

- upward advocacy is not sufficient evidence of management quality;
- people on the team directly experience management conditions that senior
  leaders may not observe;
- trust, clarity, safety, fairness, development, and sustainable work are
  relevant management evidence;
- contradictory evidence should be investigated rather than averaged away;
- team-health signals need context, privacy, and corroboration;
- positive reputation cannot erase recurring team concerns;
- team experience and operating outcomes are related but not identical.

## Remove

- a second or "downward" M score;
- formulas that multiply team sentiment into an advocacy score;
- fixed promotion gates and rates;
- claims that team-health evidence always overrides other evidence;
- claims that survey scores directly equal manager quality;
- claims that one departure is the strongest negative signal regardless of
  stated reason;
- claims that silence indicates trust, advocacy, or opposition;
- fixed behavioral signal times;
- advice to infer private manager relationships or former-report outcomes;
- covert skip-level investigation or pressure on reports to advocate upward;
- generated vignettes or panels presented as empirical validation.

## Public rewrite thesis

Team health is the team's current ability to coordinate, speak up, learn, carry
sustainable work, and receive fair support.

Use three separate evidence records:

1. observable management acts;
2. team experience;
3. operating context and outcomes.

M/S/E remains a profile of advocacy evidence. Team experience must not become a
hidden multiplier, promotion gate, or popularity score.

When records disagree:

- protect people who provide feedback;
- investigate specificity, recurrence, affected groups, and context;
- distinguish ordinary development feedback from misconduct;
- give the manager enough actionable information and a fair response path;
- assign organization-level responsibility where appropriate;
- record uncertainty rather than forcing a verdict.

## External comparison

Public research supports adjacent claims:

- psychological safety is associated with learning, voice, and performance;
- silence is ambiguous and may reflect fear, futility, or lack of opportunity;
- trust includes willingness to accept vulnerability and is not the same as
  liking or agreement;
- voluntary turnover has many individual, job, manager, and organizational
  antecedents;
- multisource feedback can support development, but effects are modest and
  implementation-dependent;
- confidentiality and small-group privacy require explicit safeguards.

No public source reviewed validates a hidden team-trust score, automatic
promotion rule, or single-signal diagnosis.
