# Module 06 Private Source Audit

This is private operating context. Do not copy private provenance, generated
statistics, employer terminology, named individuals, or identifying examples
into LIFT.

## Source concepts reviewed

- position compared with movement;
- momentum, velocity, acceleration, trajectory, and decay;
- organizational friction and active resistance;
- manager changes, reorganizations, and other resets;
- stalled and deteriorating pathways;
- response choices under structural constraints.

## Retain through rewrite

- a static M/S/E profile does not show whether evidence is strengthening,
  holding, weakening, or resetting;
- recent, repeated, more direct, and more resilient evidence can indicate
  improving pathways;
- stalled evidence and structural constraints require different responses from
  simple lack of awareness;
- reorganizations, role changes, and manager changes can alter the relevance of
  prior evidence without erasing it;
- repeated reasonable effort should have a stop condition;
- some constraints require route redesign, formal correction, transfer, or
  exit rather than indefinite effort intensification;
- active blocking behavior should be distinguished from ordinary delay,
  legitimate counterevidence, and uncertainty.

## Remove

- momentum, velocity, friction, or resistance formulas;
- subtraction or aggregation of ordinal M/S/E score changes;
- promotion timing and probability predictions;
- claims that momentum makes an outcome inevitable;
- fixed state thresholds and cycle counts;
- a scalar anti-gravity or resistance score;
- remote-work, organizational-size, or manager-pattern coefficients;
- contamination timelines and escape windows;
- signal batteries that infer coordinated opposition from ambiguous events;
- claims that a particular score means exit is the only path;
- advice to investigate private conversations through gossip;
- attribution of motive without observable evidence.

## Public rewrite thesis

Momentum is a sustained improvement in the quality, reach, or resilience of
relevant advocacy evidence over time.

Resistance is an observed act, decision, or repeated pattern that blocks,
reverses, or discounts relevant evidence. It may reflect legitimate
counterevidence, structural conflict, bias, retaliation, or other causes. Motive
must not be inferred automatically.

Track movement separately for M, S, and E:

- strengthening;
- holding;
- stalled;
- weakening;
- resetting;
- mixed or unclear.

Use event and evidence records, not score arithmetic.

## External comparison

Public research supports adjacent claims:

- momentum in organizational change is dynamic and can be reinforced or
  disrupted;
- resistance to change may provide information and may be shaped by the change
  process, not merely malicious opposition;
- reactions to change are multidimensional and context-dependent;
- career shocks can disrupt trajectories and require adaptation;
- feedback seeking is contextual and can carry power or safety costs;
- ordinal scales do not automatically support interval arithmetic.

No public source reviewed validates a career momentum formula, a resistance
score, fixed recovery curve, or promotion forecast.
