# Module 08 Private Source Audit

This is private operating context. Do not copy private provenance, generated
statistics, employer terminology, level codes, named leaders, or identifying
examples into LIFT.

## Source concepts reviewed

- manager departure and succession;
- internal transfer;
- external move and return;
- leave and workplace reentry;
- relationship and reputation portability;
- transition trajectories;
- organizational restructuring and changing authority;
- handoff, sponsorship continuity, and dependency.

## Retain through rewrite

- transitions can break some advocacy relationships while other evidence
  remains useful;
- a manager departure creates a handoff and continuity problem for the team;
- internal transfers still require adjustment and a new working contract;
- prior work, decisions, attribution, and commitments should not disappear
  during organizational change;
- the actual decision hierarchy matters more than titles;
- following a manager can preserve familiarity while increasing dependency;
- a person returning to an organization still needs current context and
  onboarding;
- transition burdens are shaped by organizational conditions, not only
  individual behavior;
- specific review triggers are more useful than passive waiting.

## Remove

- fixed arrival M/S/E profiles by entry path;
- summed "survival" thresholds;
- automatic score inheritance or numeric reset baselines;
- 90-day destiny, first-impression locks, and recovery clocks;
- generated promotion, success, compensation, and level-premium forecasts;
- claims that one prior blocker will deterministically repeat an outcome;
- score preservation based only on remaining inside the same organization;
- claims that senior placement bypasses current-manager evidence;
- advice to project a preferred leadership identity to gain score velocity;
- prestige, organization size, external visibility, or cohort arrival order as
  fixed score corrections;
- leave length as evidence of commitment, capability, or advocacy;
- private health, caregiving, family, or protected information in a handoff;
- old ratings treated as a verdict for a new manager or role.

## Public rewrite thesis

A transition changes the applicability and ownership of evidence. It does not
erase a person's history or create a justified score by formula.

The public module should:

- preserve a dated pre-transition snapshot;
- remap M/S/E to current decision authority;
- classify evidence as continuing, carried, paused, stale, retired, or unknown;
- distinguish work evidence from current advocacy;
- carry commitments with named owners and explicit disposition;
- create a privacy-bounded handoff;
- require reboarding for transfers and returns;
- protect leave from adverse commitment inference;
- make organizations responsible for continuity, not only the affected person;
- reject automatic reset values, inherited scores, and timing predictions.

## External comparison

Public research supports adjacent claims:

- role clarity, self-efficacy, and social acceptance are important parts of
  newcomer adjustment;
- both manager behavior and newcomer information seeking can shape
  socialization;
- work-role transitions involve both personal adjustment and role innovation;
- internal mobility can change the breadth and depth of work relationships;
- returning employees are not universally better or worse than other hires;
- return-to-work well-being is shaped by individual, job, and organizational
  conditions.

No public source reviewed validates LIFT transition scores, fixed reset values,
90-day destiny, score inheritance, or deterministic promotion recovery.
