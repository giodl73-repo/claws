## FRAMES findings relevant to LIFT

FRAMES explicitly separates:

- the **frame job**, such as status, coordination, momentum, risk, priority, or
  learning;
- the **story job**, such as teaching, decision, warning, repair, empathy, or
  change.

That distinction is useful for LIFT because a memorable story can otherwise
hide what it is trying to make the reader believe or do.

FRAMES also states:

> Frames explain. They do not prove.

This matches LIFT's evidence boundary.

### Necessary holds

FRAMES correctly keeps several management-derived candidates out of default
use:

- Phantom Dissonance can erase real harm or accountability.
- Advocacy Baseline can become performed care used to make criticism easier.
- Off-Axis Contact can become avoidance or side-channel manipulation.
- Reset Tax can overvalue staying in a hostile environment.
- Mentor vs. Sponsor can reduce relationships to leverage.
- Advocacy Bandwidth can rationalize favoritism or abandonment.

LIFT should not promote these merely because they are memorable.

## LUCIA findings relevant to LIFT

Useful craft patterns:

- open with one person, one place, and one bounded moment;
- let context emerge through the scene;
- preserve the subject's perspective and dignity;
- separate narrative craft from source and domain review;
- treat rubric changes as forward-only rather than rewriting old evidence.

LIFT adaptation:

- use entirely synthetic organizational scenes;
- avoid simulated testimony from protected or affected groups;
- make facts, inference, and invented illustration explicit;
- never let a vivid scene become empirical proof.

## SCENE and REEL findings relevant to LIFT

SCENE is useful when LIFT needs:

- an M/S/E pathway diagram;
- a transition or evidence-flow visualization;
- an equity/access diagram;
- checks against advocacy-as-coercion;
- a test that the visual form matches the story and underlying evidence.

REEL is useful later for:

- a short first-reader explainer;
- an animated evidence-pathway diagram;
- a scenario walkthrough;
- captioned, source-traceable public media.

Neither is needed for the core framework today.

## LIFT extraction shortlist

### 1. The bag-of-chips story

FRAMES name: `Phantom Dissonance`.

Public LIFT rule:

> When facts are incomplete, check the ownership, scarcity, threat, and intent
> model before assigning blame.

Best LIFT destinations:

- Module 01: observed, reported, inferred, and unknown evidence;
- Module 05: separate impact, intent, operating context, and repair;
- the future stories-and-metaphors companion page.

Safe story shape:

1. A person believes someone else is taking their food.
2. They interpret the interaction through that ownership assumption.
3. Later evidence shows their own food was elsewhere.
4. Their first explanation was wrong, but the tension and impact were real.

Action cue:

```text
pause accusation
  -> inspect shared facts and ownership
  -> hear the other observed view
  -> separate impact from intent
  -> choose clarification or repair
```

Evidence boundary:

- the story does not prove that nobody acted badly;
- misunderstanding is only one hypothesis;
- known harm, repeated conduct, or unequal power still requires accountability;
- once facts establish harm, stop using the empathy story and name repair,
  ownership, and the appropriate route.

Public naming recommendation:

- use the direct rule as the heading;
- optionally mention the chips story as an illustration;
- do not make `Phantom Dissonance` a person or team classification.

Do not copy the MANAGE prose. Retell only a short, generic, transformed scene.

### 2. Put the options on one surface

FRAMES name: `Board Session`.

Public LIFT rule:

> Participation is more credible when real alternatives, criteria, constraints,
> and decision authority are visible before commitment.

Best LIFT destinations:

- Module 04 transition contracts;
- inherited-distrust practice;
- manager and team decision conversations.

Required fields:

```text
Decision:
Real options:
Criteria:
Constraints:
Decision owner:
What input can still change:
Dissent or correction path:
```

Misuse warning: reject the frame when the decision is already fixed and the
board only performs inclusion.

### 3. The org chart is not the contact map

FRAMES name: `Contact Structure`.

Public LIFT rule:

> Formal reporting lines do not reveal who actually receives context, sees
> evidence, controls resources, or can correct a distorted account.

Best LIFT destinations:

- Module 03 management cascade;
- Module 09 access and equity;
- the uneven-access reader guide.

Action cue:

- map the legitimate work pathway, not private relationships;
- identify absence, delay, distortion, concentration, and dependency;
- create a safer direct or fallback route where appropriate.

Misuse warning: do not build a covert influence map, shadow personnel graph, or
relationship score.

### 4. What carries and what must be rebuilt

FRAMES name: `Portability Index`; safer source scene: luggage.

Public LIFT rule:

> A transition can carry work evidence while changing the people, authority,
> context, and advocacy paths that make the evidence usable.

Best LIFT destination:

- Module 08 transitions;
- the manager-change and environment-choice guides.

Useful categories:

```text
skills
artifacts
work evidence
context
trust
relationships
authority
commitments
```

For each, record `carries`, `partly carries`, `must be rebuilt`, or `unknown`.

Misuse warning: rebuilding cost is not an argument that a person should remain
in an unsafe or hostile environment.

### 5. Same position, different movement

FRAMES name: `Momentum State`.

This is already substantially present in Module 06. Do not add another
metaphor. At most, use one short illustration:

> Two paths can show the same current position while one is strengthening and
> the other is weakening.

Keep the existing LIFT movement states authoritative. Do not add velocity,
force, or resistance arithmetic.
