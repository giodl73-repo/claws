# Module 07 Private Source Audit

This is private operating context. Do not copy private provenance, generated
statistics, employer terminology, level codes, named leaders, or identifying
examples into LIFT.

## Source concepts reviewed

- choosing a manager and organization;
- pre-join due diligence;
- hiring and entry pathways;
- manager history and former-report outcomes;
- organizational access and visibility;
- role, compensation, and environment tradeoffs;
- remote work, career gaps, and nonlinear careers;
- equity risks in inference.

## Retain through rewrite

- candidates should inspect the manager and environment, not only title and
  compensation;
- role reality, decision authority, workload, feedback, opportunity, and
  organizational stability matter;
- specific examples are more useful than generic claims;
- comparing answers across legitimate sources can reveal uncertainty or
  inconsistency;
- entry pathway may affect initial access and expectations;
- written terms and explicit unknowns reduce avoidable expectation gaps;
- candidates have unequal leverage and may not be able to ask every question;
- organizations should provide realistic evidence proactively.

## Remove

- inference of a candidate's or manager's M/S/E from career history;
- generated hiring classifiers, accuracy claims, and corrections;
- promotion velocity, prestige, tenure, or organizational size as score
  proxies;
- deterministic entry-path score assignments and recovery timelines;
- predictions that one manager determines a candidate's outcome;
- rating managers from former-report promotions or online profiles;
- covert contact with employees or former reports;
- claims that executive involvement guarantees sponsorship;
- advice to route "high-potential" people by inferred manager score;
- fixed remote-work or nonlinear-career penalties;
- employer use of LIFT as a candidate-selection instrument.

## Public rewrite thesis

Choosing an environment is reciprocal diligence:

- the employer evaluates job-relevant candidate evidence;
- the candidate receives enough specific and honest environment evidence to
  make a decision under uncertainty.

Pre-join M/S/E is `unknown` by default. A candidate may inspect whether
advocacy pathways appear possible, but should not score private relationships
or predict outcomes.

The public module should:

- define six environment lenses;
- provide safe questions and a minimum-diligence path;
- distinguish evidence from recruitment signaling;
- encourage realistic job previews and written terms;
- make unknowns and reversibility explicit;
- prohibit online profiling and protected-characteristic inference;
- give organizations a reciprocal environment evidence brief;
- remain usable by candidates with limited leverage.

## External comparison

Public research supports adjacent claims:

- realistic job previews can modestly improve expectations and turnover
  outcomes;
- recruiter behavior, organizational image, and perceived fit influence
  applicant attraction;
- perceived fit relates to later attitudes and outcomes but is measured in
  different ways and can reinforce similarity bias;
- structured interviews improve consistency and validity compared with
  unstructured interviews;
- pre-join messages can shape psychological contracts;
- social-media screening creates privacy, relevance, and inference risks.

No public source reviewed validates pre-join M/S/E inference, manager scoring,
entry-path destiny, or career-timing predictions.
