# Module 03 Private Source Audit

This is private operating context. Do not copy private provenance, generated
statistics, employer terminology, or identifying examples into LIFT.

## Source concepts reviewed

- the management cascade;
- manager relationships as relay infrastructure;
- manager network position and organizational standing;
- team visibility through skip and senior forums;
- organizational scale and administrative exposure;
- manager departure and continuity risk;
- context effects on access to advocacy.

## Retain through rewrite

- a manager's own information and relationship pathways can affect a team's
  access to context, resources, credit, opportunity, and sponsorship;
- strong local management does not guarantee that evidence travels beyond the
  team;
- weak pathways can create a bottleneck even when the manager advocates
  intensely;
- teams need direct and redundant evidence paths rather than permanent
  dependence on one manager;
- organizational assignment can create exposure without creating sponsorship;
- manager departure can reveal fragile, single-person pathways;
- structural access problems require system redesign, not simply more effort
  from the affected person.

## Remove

- claims that a manager's score predicts almost all report outcomes;
- manager-to-report score multipliers, correlations, and point lifts;
- formulas that cap a report's S or E below the manager's score;
- claims that a manager pattern is reproduced deterministically in reports;
- fixed time-to-promotion and gate-readiness predictions;
- claims that changing manager is the only remedy;
- numerical organizational-size formulas for executive advocacy;
- fixed remote-work score penalties;
- claims that administrative exposure creates a score baseline;
- claims that a manager's departure causes a predictable numerical reset;
- generated studies described as validated empirical research.

## Public rewrite thesis

A management cascade is the way context, evidence, resources, opportunity, and
relationships move through a manager's organizational pathways.

The manager may amplify, attenuate, delay, distort, concentrate, or interrupt
those flows. The manager is not the only path and does not determine another
person's future.

The public module should help teams:

- map actual pathways rather than infer private relationships;
- distinguish exposure from advocacy;
- identify single-person dependencies;
- create direct and redundant routes;
- preserve accurate evidence through transitions;
- inspect unequal access without scoring network size or political activity.

## External comparison

Public research supports adjacent claims:

- team boundary management can improve performance, with effects depending on
  activity, target, carrier, and context;
- team and leader network structures are associated with team viability and
  performance;
- external activity can help or harm depending on its form;
- boundary spanning can create coordination, stress, and overload costs;
- sponsorship access can reproduce inequality.

No public source reviewed validates a manager-to-report score formula, a hard
advocacy ceiling, or deterministic pattern inheritance.
