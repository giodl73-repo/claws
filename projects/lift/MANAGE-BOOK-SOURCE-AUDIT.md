# MANAGE Book Private Source Audit

This is private operating context. Do not copy names, employers, buildings,
products, dates, quotations, career outcomes, organizational structures,
private stories, or generated reviews into public LIFT.

Source reviewed:

```text
C:\src\rmm\book\MANAGE
```

The manuscript calls the book `SIGNAL` and frames it as thirty years of
management experience mapped to the RESONANCE formula.

## Verdict

`MANAGE` is suitable as a private conceptual and narrative source for LIFT.

It is not suitable for:

- direct inclusion in the LIFT repository;
- direct public release under LIFT;
- becoming another LIFT module;
- preserving its current formula claims, named patterns, cases, or generated
  reviews.

Recommended boundary:

```text
MANAGE narrative source
  -> LIFT evidence, ethical-use, and publication filters
  -> a small number of substantially rewritten practices

MANAGE / RESONANCE formula and book
  -> remain a separate work
```

## Why it is not a LIFT book

LIFT is an evidence-linked framework for advocacy and organizational pathways.

`MANAGE` is a narrative management book governed by a different ontology:

```text
tension = observation x dissonance
```

Merging the formula, named patterns, and narrative cases into LIFT would:

- create a competing core model;
- turn LIFT into a general management theory;
- reintroduce pattern classification;
- blur evidence labels;
- encourage unsupported causal inference;
- weaken the safety-before-score boundary;
- duplicate practices LIFT already expresses more safely.

## Manuscript-level risks

### Re-identification

The manuscript includes combinations of:

- employer and campus;
- buildings and dates;
- named products and technologies;
- organizational levels;
- named or recognizable people;
- direct quotations;
- promotions, hiring, departures, reorgs, and career outcomes;
- customer or partner geography;
- exact organizational arrangements.

Changing names alone would not sanitize these stories.

### Evidence

The prologue claims that an organizational formula is the same mathematical
structure seen in chemistry, signal processing, epidemiology, and population
biology.

The manuscript itself acknowledges uncertainty about analogy versus common
phenomenon, then says the equation "governs" organizational observation.

The book's own review synthesis identifies the central gap:

- scientific rhetorical posture;
- selected personal cases;
- insufficient evidence for derivation, causality, and generality.

For LIFT, the formula must be treated as a separate synthetic model or
practitioner metaphor, not an established law.

### Employment decisions

Several chapters include or imply:

- promotion readiness and timing;
- performance classification;
- feedback-refusal trajectories;
- hiring portfolio judgments;
- candidate growth inference;
- career ceilings;
- managed exits;
- replacement signals;
- relationship portfolios for career decisions.

These conflict with LIFT's prohibited employment-decision boundary.

### Paranoia and inference

The final chapter encourages readers to decode:

- executive vocabulary;
- changes in warmth;
- pre-announcement behavior;
- hidden organizational decisions;
- whether a person has sufficient stakes in another person's career.

This risks mind-reading, covert relationship mapping, self-surveillance, and
unsafe action. LIFT should retain only observable evidence, counterevidence,
unknowns, and safe clarification.

### Generated review

The manuscript includes synthetic reviews written in the names or styles of
well-known authors and thinkers.

These are internal writing lenses. They cannot be presented as real reviews,
endorsements, independent experts, or empirical evidence.

## Possible new LIFT material

Only two concepts appear sufficiently distinct to test as future reader guides:

### Inherited distrust

Potential question:

> What should a manager do when distrust was created before they arrived?

Safe public rewrite:

- acknowledge that distrust may be earned and structurally inherited;
- do not demand immediate trust;
- start with bounded, work-relevant commitments;
- let the team contest the manager's model;
- avoid forced personal disclosure or team-building;
- review management acts, team experience, and context separately.

Likely home:

- Module 04;
- Module 05;
- a new reader guide only if independent readers cannot find the practice.

### Layered contact

Potential question:

> How do people with different models approach a complex decision without
> starting at the most contested implementation detail?

Safe public rewrite:

```text
purpose
  -> user or stakeholder need
  -> decision criteria
  -> constraints
  -> implementation
```

This is broader communication practice and may fit better in a separate
management-writing project than in LIFT.

## Extraction decision

Decision date: 2026-08-07

No Module 11 and no tenth reader guide were created.

The smallest coherent addition was made through the existing owner surfaces:

- Module 04 now treats inherited distrust as transition context and adds a
  bounded sequence for acknowledgement, participation, commitment, and
  follow-through.
- Module 05 now separates inherited distrust from a verdict about the new
  manager and adds explicit no-reset, no-compelled-disclosure, and
  no-guaranteed-repair boundaries.
- The existing starting-as-a-manager guide now makes the practice
  discoverable without expanding the guide library.
- A synthetic example demonstrates three separate records without claiming
  that trust has returned.
- Layered contact was retained only as a small conversation sequence inside the
  transition practice, not as a new LIFT model.

The addition used adjacent public trust-repair research and labels the
operational sequence as LIFT practice. It does not import the RESONANCE formula,
MANAGE pattern names, employer stories, or employment-decision guidance.

## Publication recommendation

### For LIFT

Do not import the book.

Use this audit to:

1. confirm existing module coverage;
2. test inherited-distrust as a possible guide;
3. consider layered contact only if it solves a demonstrated LIFT usability
   gap;
4. keep every narrative case synthetic and substantially transformed;
5. reject all employment-decision, hidden-signal, and relationship-portfolio
   material.

### For the book

If `MANAGE` is prepared for public release, keep it separate from LIFT and run
its own:

- story-by-story consent and re-identification audit;
- employer and product sanitization;
- claim ledger;
- public-source review;
- synthetic-review labeling or removal;
- labor, legal, privacy, and harm review;
- formula downgrade from law to testable metaphor unless evidence supports
  more;
- title and repository-name review because `SIGNAL` collides with existing
  public repositories.

## Bottom line

`MANAGE` contains strong management writing and several useful practices.

LIFT already contains most of its safe operational value.

The book should remain a separate narrative work, while LIFT may selectively
learn from two areas:

- inherited distrust;
- layered contact.
