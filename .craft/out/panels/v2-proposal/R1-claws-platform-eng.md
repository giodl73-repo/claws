# R1 panel review — claws:platform-eng — v2 OpenClaw contribution proposal

**Reviewer**: claws:platform-eng (Operator; Cluster D; "Fleet consistency > Workspace autonomy")
**Round**: 1
**Subject**: snap-pre-rfc.md, policy-pre-rfc.md, guardrails-adapter.md, realigned-story.md
**Date**: 2026-04-28
**Lens**: contract-chain composition, sequencing/circularity, upstream-shape conformance, multi-tenant/multi-binary, drift surface, fleet-broadcast / drift / doctor reachability.

---

## TL;DR — the verdict up top

**APPROVE-WITH-WARNINGS** with three Major items that must be resolved before any pre-RFC opens, and a class of Warnings about contract-chain composition that the four documents wave at but don't actually pin down.

The architectural shape is correct — yankee, pundit, and bandit each independently arrive at "small contract upstream, daemon stays downstream," and the realigned-story is the right knit. The 0 LoC guardrails finding is real. The trim arithmetic mostly holds. **But the four documents disagree internally about three load-bearing facts, and one of those disagreements (the path constant) is the type-level keystone of the entire chain.** The chain is also sketched at the type level only; under any deployment shape that splits domains across binaries (Eduardo's filing model, the multi-tenant case), the SnapshotStore contract as drafted assumes in-process access and silently breaks. Finally, the cross-contract concern weaver flagged about `evaluate(event, ctx)` not carrying snapshot ID has a workaround that's documented but not analyzed for correctness — and it's wrong in the multi-process case.

These are fixable. None require re-architecting; all require pinning down a fact the docs leave fuzzy. Fix the three Majors before opening any pre-RFC; the Warnings can be addressed in PR-description text or follow-on.

## Cross-contract concerns — pushing on weaver's flagged four

weaver flagged 4 concerns at realigned-story.md:174-179. Refining:

1. **R0–R5 tier labels claws-specific** — Correctly flagged. The fix (re-frame as generic `Sensitivity` enum) is small and unambiguous. **No further refinement.**
2. **HostContext / ProviderLifecycle asymmetry** — Correctly flagged but under-analyzed. The proposed fix ("captured by closure at construction") is correct for the single-tenant single-process case; in multi-tenant, the closure-captured `HostContext` is per-tenant and the upstream `evaluate(event, ctx)` doesn't carry tenant. **This is W6** — the closure pattern works only if exactly one composed provider serves all tenants, which is not generally true.
3. **`evaluate(event, ctx)` may not carry snapshot ID** — Correctly flagged. **The proposed workaround is wrong in multi-process.** This is **M2** — needs upstream extension, not downstream adapter.
4. **Snap × PolicyIR conformance test couples PRs** — Correctly flagged. **The proposed sequencing reinforces the coupling rather than breaking it.** This is **W3** — reverse the order, land PolicyIR first.

**Concerns weaver missed:**

5. **Path constant divergence (M1)** — three different paths across four documents.
6. **`OcPath` type mismatch (M1, W2)** — structured interface vs string brand vs workspace identifier; three senses confused into one type.
7. **Trim arithmetic double-count (M3)** — `claws-sdk/src/audit/` lands in three different destinations across the audits.
8. **Fleet-scope absence (W4)** — drift / broadcast / doctor have no contract surface.
9. **Tenant-scope absence (W6)** — multi-tenant deployments unaddressed.
10. **Snap's lost `current()` (W5)** — yankee → pre-RFC simplification dropped a method whose role in the consumption flow wasn't replaced.

Concerns 5–10 are not visible from the type-level chain inspection alone; they emerge when you compose the chain against actual consumers (drift, doctor, fleet-comply, multi-tenant gateways). weaver's view from inside the realigned-story is the right one for what it covers; it doesn't extend into the operational consumers.

## Architectural-coherence summary

The chain `oc-path → snap → PolicyIR → guardrails-adapter` composes at the type level **only if M1 is fixed** (the path-constant + OcPath sense). With M1 fixed, snap depends on oc-path, PolicyIR depends on opaque `SnapshotId` (so it can land independently), guardrails-adapter depends on PolicyIR + already-shipped `registerTrustedToolPolicy`. Clean DAG, no circularity at the type level.

At the runtime level, the chain composes for **single-process single-tenant** deployments. For Eduardo's split-by-domain or multi-tenant deployments (M2, W4, W6), it does not. The pre-RFCs should either explicitly scope to the single-process single-tenant case (a defensible v1) or extend the upstream contracts to carry the required scoping (snapshot ID in ctx; tenant ID in workspace identifier).

The 0-LoC-guardrails finding is sound and the proposal correctly leverages an existing upstream surface. The trim arithmetic is **directionally correct but not pin-able** at the headline LoC until M3 resolves.

The contract-shape compatibility with the three reference packages (`memory-host-sdk`, `plugin-package-contract`, `plugin-sdk`) is structural-correct: types-only, no daemon, plugin-mediated runtime. The reference plugin's `auth: 'gateway'` requires W1 acknowledgment for the non-gateway-host case.

## Recommendation

**APPROVE-WITH-WARNINGS.**

The architectural framing is right. The discipline of audits → realigned-story → three pre-RFCs is the right shape. The 250-500 LoC upstream surface is achievable and matches Peter's pattern.

But **do not open any pre-RFC until M1, M2, M3 are resolved.** They are individually fixable:

- **M1** (~30 minutes of editing): pick a path, align all four docs, decide what `OcPath` is for the contract.
- **M2** (~1 sprint of #clawtributors thread): get Peter's directional read on extending `PluginHookToolContext` with `snapshotId?: string`. If yes, the chain works multi-process. If no, scope the proposal explicitly.
- **M3** (~30 minutes of editing): pick the `claws-sdk/src/audit/` destination, recompute the headline LoC, document the post-trim package boundary.

W3 (sequencing reverse) is also a 30-minute fix and worth doing before opening; it eliminates the conjoined-twin PR problem.

W4, W5, W6 are larger but can land as PR-description text or follow-on issues if the team commits to scoping v1 to single-process single-tenant.
